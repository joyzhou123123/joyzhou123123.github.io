Platform Definition

Then grounding the solution in a deployable AI-desktop platform.

When AI workflows need to run long, coordinate multiple models, and process private data, the product needs a stable, purchasable, maintainable physical carrier. I defined form factor, expansion, cooling/maintenance, and under-desk placement together · so the solution moves from concept to the real office floor.

01

Back-derive platform capability from target users

Focused on creators, developers, AI research, and enterprise knowledge workers. They care about local 70B post-training, 397B LLM runs, long-task stability, desktop usability, and on-prem data retention · product value unfolds from the complete workflow.

02

Re-architect the form factor with vertical airflow

Traditional towers depend on front-back airflow, where vented faces dictate looks, placement, and maintenance. My definition switched the airflow to top-intake / bottom-exhaust, uniting four hole-free faces, under-desk fit, and reachable top I/O into one new form.

03

Turn expansion, cooling, and maintenance into deployment trust

Dual GPUs, dual NICs, GPU-specific brackets, tool-free dust filters, blocked-airflow desktop alerts, and repeated thermal/acoustic testing support local large-model compute, maintenance-risk control, and longer-lifecycle enterprise value.

04

Reserve a product entry for the natural-interaction layer

Once the AI desktop becomes a workflow node, the next layer of value is making the system understand people and space. Sensor Hub is defined as the sensing, feedback, and natural-interaction entry · the last mile of the local AI workflow.
